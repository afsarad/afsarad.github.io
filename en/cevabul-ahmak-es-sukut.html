Islam through Questions

When the maxim of answering foolishness with silence may be set aside

Adem Afşar

Why I am opening this section

In this section I want to examine claims made on social media about the Qur’an, Islam and the Prophet Muhammad. I will focus particularly on arguments that appear contradictory at first sight, leave readers with unanswered questions or lead to misleading conclusions through incomplete accounts. By presenting my own thinking and its reasons, I hope to help readers reconsider these matters.

I use the expression in the title as a maxim here; I do not present it as a Qur’anic verse or a hadith. I do not use the word “foolish” to describe everyone who asks a question. To understand an objection, we first need to examine what the person says and the reasons they offer.

Silence towards whom and with what outcome

Sometimes ending an argument can prevent insults and anger from escalating. On social media, however, the person posting a statement is not its only reader. Others may see it and feel confused because they do not know how to answer. This is the distinction I want to explore: we can stop an unproductive exchange with one person while continuing to explain the claim to those who read it.

For this reason, I do not think we must join the argument beneath every post. We can describe the claim clearly on our own page and examine its foundations. A response can also address readers seeking to understand the issue. In deciding whether to respond, I will ask what our explanation can clarify.

When I consider an explanation necessary

I consider an explanation worthwhile when part of a verse is quoted while its continuation is withheld; a translation choice is presented as the text’s only possible meaning; a statement whose source cannot be found is attributed to the Prophet; or an individual’s conduct is used to draw conclusions about the entire religion. These are types of claim we will examine, rather than advance judgements about a particular post.

If we encounter a difficulty that genuinely needs investigation, I will state it plainly too. To establish that a claim is invalid, we need to show what is wrong with its premises, information or conclusion. I also want to distinguish explaining my own faith from offering shared evidence to someone who begins from different assumptions.

How we will examine each claim

First, we will convey the claim without changing its meaning and record the post’s source and date. We will then identify the verse, transmitted report, historical information or reasoning on which it relies. After examining the full text and context, we will ask whether the claim’s conclusion actually follows from that information.

My own thinking will appear under a separate heading. The Qur’anic text, hadith reports, explanations in Risale-i Nur and my own assessment will remain distinguishable. When drawing on Risale-i Nur, we will identify the work and relevant section. We will not present wording we cannot verify as the source’s own statement.

Responsibility in responding

A misleading claim does not mean we know with certainty the intentions of the person sharing it. We will not build our examination around judging a person’s worth or inner life. Humiliating someone who asks a sincere question undermines the purpose of offering an explanation.

My aim in this section is to help people with questions about their faith see the issue more clearly. We will look for the strength of our answers in their reasoning. Correcting an error in my own assessment is part of the same responsibility.
