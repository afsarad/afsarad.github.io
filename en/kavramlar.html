Subjective Benefit Creation

A conceptual proposition by Adem Afşar

“People can derive a benefit, from their own point of view, not only from what they gain but also from what they give up, the values they uphold, the choices they make, and the costs they accept. Social relationships take shape as these differing pursuits of benefit encounter one another.”

A benefactor may find satisfaction in having a school or fountain named after them; another may feel satisfied when their wish to remain unnamed is respected. Opposite choices can each carry subjective value for the person making them.

This proposition does not claim to prove that every action is a conscious calculation of self-interest or that every choice necessarily produces pleasure. It offers a framework for examining the subjective meanings of action and the relationship between individual benefit and social order.

— Adem Afşar
