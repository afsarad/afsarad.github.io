The Book's Central Question

Can the Muqaddimah be read not merely as a historical chronicle explaining why individual states collapsed, but as a structural theory for understanding the formation, strengthening, prosperity and dissolution of political sovereignty?

This study distinguishes dynastic change from the disappearance of society. Conquest, regime change, ideological dissolution and state fragmentation are not identical events. As one sovereignty ends, people, cultures and institutions may persist to varying degrees.

Method

Placing a historical event within a sequence of formation, strengthening, prosperity and dissolution does not alone explain its cause. The book therefore separately evaluates, in each example, the political structure dissolving, the social elements retaining continuity and the phenomena the theory can or cannot explain.

Proximal responsibility, here, is an interpretive concept concerning geographical, linguistic and institutional limits on access to knowledge. The world Ibn Khaldun knew directly differs from the world in which his theory can be tested. Distant civilisations such as China therefore constitute independent fields of comparison.
