As Petrol Excise Rises, How Much Time Does the State Give People to Adapt?

Adem Afşar
Interdisciplinary sociological analyst
28 November 2026

Excise duty on certain petrol grades will rise on 1 December 2026. For the 95-octane unleaded petrol covered by the decision, the duty will increase from TRY 11.36 to TRY 14.8277 per litre. The roughly TRY 3.47 difference is the change in duty. The change at the pump will become clear when retail prices are set. Presidential Decision No. 11822, published on 1 October, announced the December rate.

Why did the government phase out the sliding excise scheme?

The sliding scheme reduced excise duty when refinery prices rose, absorbing part of the increase for consumers through lower tax receipts. Under the phase-out schedule adopted in July, tax reductions absorbed 50% of refinery price increases through July and 25% in August and September. The scheme ended on 1 October.

The Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye says petrol excise will return to its pre-scheme level in December 2026 and diesel excise in January 2027. In the bank’s assessment, phasing in the tax changes prevented them from reaching pump prices all at once.

From a public-finance perspective, the tax reduction transfers part of a price increase to the public budget through lower receipts. Restoring the previous rate increases the tax per litre. The Treasury and Finance Ministry reported a central government deficit of approximately TRY 2.32 trillion for the first ten months of 2026. This gives fiscal context for the importance of revenue. The official announcements examined do not identify the deficit as the reason for the decision.

How does the global oil market affect this picture?

Reuters reported that Brent futures closed at USD 95.44 per barrel on 27 November. Brent fell on Friday but rose around 3% over the week. Attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz and on Saudi energy infrastructure raised supply concerns. International oil and fuel prices and exchange rates matter alongside excise duty for pump prices in Türkiye. Late-November volatility cannot explain the decision announced in October.

Adem Afşar’s sociological analysis

Long-term, stable governance can give people time to plan and a reasonable expectation of reliable information about what lies ahead. A family wants to budget for winter heating, a worker for next month’s commute and a shopkeeper for deliveries. Reliable planning depends on institutions making clear when a change will take effect, how long support will last and what they will do if conditions shift. In everyday life, institutional stability shows in whether people can rely on announced rules and timetables.

A welfare state has several tools. Covering part of a gas bill reduces heating costs. Sliding excise can absorb part of a rise in external costs. Cheaper fares reduce expenditure for people who take the bus or metro. Each tool reaches a different group. A night-shift worker can benefit from a fare reduction only if a service runs to their neighbourhood at that hour.

Consider two workers at the same factory on the same wage. One works days and can take a bus home. The other drives because there is no service after the night shift. Both can learn the tax timetable two months ahead. The day worker can drive on fewer days. The night worker may need an employer shuttle or a municipal service at the right hour. Advance notice gives both time. Their ability to use that time differs.

Assessing the phase-out as social policy requires asking how much the scheme cushioned higher prices, how much adjustment time it gave people and who bore the remaining costs. A tax timetable helps the state manage its budget. Households also need practical alternatives if they are to act on that information.
